What Lies Inside Vault B? | EP 1

The Sealed Door

You might think you know the value of the Padmanabhaswamy Temple, but you have never heard of the thirty crore undeciphered records. I am Itihaas Dada, and tonight we descend into the real history behind the headlines.

Myths and Serpents

For years, wild rumors have turned this ancient shrine in Keralam into a playground for folklore. People whispered of deadly cobras guarding the threshold, and curses waiting for anyone who dared turn a key. Stories claimed that opening Vault B would unleash floods or divine wrath upon the land.

Ancient Foundations

Let us step away from the ghost stories and look at what the actual stone and parchment tell us. Dedicated to Vishnu, the temple is one of the one hundred eight sacred Divya Desams of the Sri Vaishnava tradition. Tamil Sangam literature and ninth-century poet-saints praised this shrine as a heavenly abode. Long before modern courts arrived, the temple was governed by an assembly called the Ettara Yogam.

The Royal Decree

In the eighteenth century, King Anizham Thirunal Marthanda Varma reshaped the destiny of Travancore. On January seventeenth, seventeen fifty, he surrendered his entire kingdom to the deity. From that day onward, the rulers governed not as kings, but as humble servants named Padmanabha Dasa.

Vaults Unearthed

Centuries passed until the Supreme Court of India ordered an inventory of the temple underground. In June twenty-and-eleven, inspection teams opened several underground vaults for the first time. What they found inside stunned the nation and rewrote the scale of temple treasures.

The Wealth Revealed

Vaults A, C, D, E, and F yielded mountains of gold coins, diamond necklaces, and antique statues. Historians estimated the total discovered value at roughly two lakh crore rupees. Yet, amid all this shimmering gold, Vault B remained completely untouched.

Vault B Mystery

Why was Vault B left sealed while the other chambers were thoroughly documented? The royal family and temple traditionalists insisted that opening Vault B violated sacred protocol. Legal petitions flew back and forth through the courts of Thiruvananthapuram and New Delhi.

Legal Standoff

The Supreme Court eventually ruled that the Travancore Royal Family could resume temple administration. On July third, twenty-twenty, the court overturned earlier rulings giving control back to the descendants. However, the judicial order maintained strict protection over the sacred sanctity of the shrine.

Beyond the Legends

So what is actually behind those iron doors of Vault B today? Archaeologists suggest it may contain more ancient offerings accumulated over a thousand years. There are no supernatural serpents guarding the chamber, only centuries of uncounted history.

The Twelve Thousand Stones

Look closer at the principal deity inside the sanctum. The massive form of Padmanabha reclining on the serpent Anantha is not carved from a single quarry block. Instead, the deity is constructed from exactly twelve thousand and eight saligramams, sacred fossil stones brought all the way from the banks of the Gandaki River in Nepal. King Marthanda Varma imported these holy stones in seventeen thirty-one to re-consecrate the shrine. Then his workers covered the entire assembly in a special ayurvedic protective mix called Katusarkara yogam.

The Mathilakam Records

How do we know any of this with such precision? In fourteen-twenty-five, King Veera Iravi Iravi Varma ordered the construction of a record-room right inside the temple compound. This granta-pura stored the Mathilakam records. Over three thousand cadjan leaf bundles containing tens of thousands of individual administrative entries were kept here across centuries. Archaeologist R. Nagaswamy noted that these documents total over thirty crore records. Yet, a vast majority of these ancient Middle Tamil and Malayalam bundles remain undeciphered today.

The Marauder at the Gate

The temple wealth also made it a target for outside forces. In sixteen-eighty, a military leader named Mukilan invaded parts of Venad with the explicit intent to plunder and destroy the shrine. But the attack failed completely. Local Muslims who were loyal to the royal house of Venad actively dissuaded him from desecrating the holy site. Mukilan was killed shortly after in the Battle of Manacaud. The temple survived intact, preserving its treasures and its daily rituals through the shifting tides of history.

The Golden Hymns of the Saints

Long before stone walls rose to secure the perimeter, poets walked these very streets. Tamil Sangam literature from the early centuries of the common era explicitly praised what they called the Golden Temple. By the seventh and eighth centuries, poet-saints known as the Alwars were singing hymns of pure devotion. Nammalwar eulogized this shrine as heaven itself, describing city streets paved in gold.

The Thirty Crores of Records

How do we verify these grand accounts? In fourteen-twenty-five, King Veera Iravi Iravi Varma ordered a dedicated record-room built within the temple walls. Over three thousand fragile cadjan leaf bundles were later moved to state archives. Archaeologist R. Nagaswamy noted that each leaf documents ten thousand entries, totaling over thirty crores of individual records.

The River of Saligrams

The sacred idol inside the sanctum holds its own geological mystery. It is constructed from exactly twelve thousand and eight saligramams. These aniconic stones were brought all the way from the banks of the Gandaki River in Nepal. To seal them together, craftsmen applied an ancient Ayurvedic herbal mix known as Katusarkara yogam.

The Seven Potti Families

Long before kings built stone bastions, legends say the sage Parashurama himself purified the sacred idol here in the Dvapara Yuga. Parashurama entrusted the administration of the temple to seven specific Potti families. Names like Koopakkara Potti and Karuva Potti became the first guardians of the shrine. He also directed King Adithya Vikrama of Venad to protect the temple, setting a precedent of royal duty that would last for centuries.

The Divine Charioteer

The main shrine is not the only sacred space within these walls. Two other important shrines stand independently inside the complex. One is Thiruvambadi, dedicated to Krishna Swami as Parthasarathi, the divine charioteer of Arjuna from the Mahabharata. On special days like Ekadashi, the deity is dressed and adorned as Mohini, maintaining rituals that have never missed a single day.

The Thirty Crores of Records

How do we know any of this with such precision? In fourteen-twenty-five, King Veera Iravi Iravi Varma ordered a dedicated record-room built within the temple walls. Over three thousand fragile cadjan leaf bundles were later moved to the State Archives Department in eighteen-sixty-seven. According to historian R. Nagaswamy, each bundle contains up to ten thousand documentations, totaling over thirty crores of historical records.

The Marauder at the Gate

The immense wealth of the temple naturally drew dangerous attention. In sixteen-eighty, a military leader named Mukilan invaded parts of Venad. His explicit intent was to plunder and destroy the Padmanabhaswamy Temple, just as he had destroyed nearby shrines. But local Muslims loyal to the royals of Venad dissuaded him, and Mukilan was killed shortly after in the Battle of Manacaud.

The Unbroken Continuity

Today, only those who profess the Hindu faith are permitted entry, and devotees must strictly follow traditional dress codes. Men wear the vesti and angavastram in plain white, while women wear traditional saris, stepping back into another century.
